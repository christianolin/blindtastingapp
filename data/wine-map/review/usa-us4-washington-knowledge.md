## United States › Washington › Columbia Valley › Ancient Lakes of Columbia Valley

Name: **Ancient Lakes of Columbia Valley** · key `united-states.washington.columbia-valley.ancient-lakes-of-columbia-valley` · AVA

**Description.** An AVA of 162,762 acres in central Washington around the town of Quincy, in Douglas, Grant and Kittitas counties, with the Columbia River along its western edge. It is named for the lakes that dot its ice-age flood channels. It is one of the few Washington AVAs planted more to white grapes than to red, above all Riesling.

**Climate.** One of the more northerly parts of the Columbia Valley, with many vineyards a little higher than the valley around them, so heat builds later in the season and fades sooner. Wide swings between day and night help the grapes keep their acidity, and the Columbia River shields many sites from early and late frost.

**Soils.** Soils left by the Missoula Floods, mostly fine sand with silt and sandy loam; some sites, such as Evergreen Vineyard, have deposits of calcium-rich caliche. Most of the area slopes gently, at under 4%, toward its eastern boundary.

**Grape varieties (text).** Riesling leads the plantings, with Chardonnay and Sauvignon Blanc among the more than 20 varieties grown.

**Wine styles (text).** Mostly white wines, led by Riesling, Chardonnay and Sauvignon Blanc, with some red wines.

**Key facts**

- Established 2012 (27 CFR 9.227)
- About 162,762 acres, with six commercial vineyards on about 1,399 acres at approval (T.D. TTB-108)
- Douglas, Grant and Kittitas counties
- Part of the Channeled Scablands, carved by the ice-age floods

## United States › Washington › Columbia Valley › Horse Heaven Hills

Name: **Horse Heaven Hills** · key `united-states.washington.columbia-valley.horse-heaven-hills` · AVA

**Description.** A large AVA in south-central Washington, in Benton, Klickitat and Yakima counties, rising north from the Columbia River. Its name comes from an early pioneer who, seeing its wide prairies, called it horse heaven. About a quarter of Washington's wine-grape acreage is planted here, much of it on south-facing slopes.

**Climate.** Warm and windy, with an arid to semi-arid continental climate, so vineyards are irrigated. Steady winds keep canopies small and grape skins thick and discourage mold and rot, and the Columbia River tempers the risk of early and late frost.

**Soils.** Three main soil types, all well drained: wind-blown sand and loess, Missoula Flood sediment, and rubble washed down the hill slopes from the Columbia River basalt bedrock.

**Grape varieties (text).** Cabernet Sauvignon makes up a large share of plantings, with Merlot, Chardonnay and Riesling.

**Wine styles (text).** Red wines led by Cabernet Sauvignon and Merlot, and white wines from Chardonnay and Riesling.

**Key facts**

- Established 2005 (27 CFR 9.188)
- About 570,000 acres, about 17,082 under vine (Washington State Wine Commission)
- Benton, Klickitat and Yakima counties
- First vinifera planted in 1972, at what is now Champoux Vineyard

## United States › Washington › Columbia Valley › Lake Chelan

Name: **Lake Chelan** · key `united-states.washington.columbia-valley.lake-chelan` · AVA

**Description.** An AVA of about 24,040 acres wrapped around the southern end of Lake Chelan in north-central Washington, in Chelan County, within the Columbia Valley AVA. Unlike most of the Columbia Valley it sits on granitic rather than basalt bedrock, and it lies north of the reach of the Missoula Floods. Its first modern vinifera plantings date from 1998.

**Climate.** The glacier-carved lake, up to 1,486 feet deep, moderates the climate: summer days are cooler and summer nights warmer than in the land around it. Vineyards lie along the southern and eastern shores at about 1,100 feet, higher than many southern parts of the Columbia Valley.

**Soils.** Glacial sediments with a good deal of quartz and mica, mixed with ash and pumice from the volcanoes of the Cascade Range.

**Grape varieties (text).** Syrah and Pinot Noir lead, with Chardonnay, Pinot Gris, Merlot and Riesling.

**Wine styles (text).** Red wines from Syrah, Pinot Noir and Merlot, and white wines from Chardonnay, Pinot Gris and Riesling.

**Key facts**

- Established 2009 (27 CFR 9.215)
- About 24,040 acres, about 269 under vine (Washington State Wine Commission)
- Chelan County
- Parts of its boundary follow the 2,000-foot elevation line (27 CFR 9.215)

## United States › Washington › Columbia Valley › Naches Heights

Name: **Naches Heights** · key `united-states.washington.columbia-valley.naches-heights` · AVA

**Description.** An AVA of about 13,254 acres on a plateau northwest of Yakima, between the towns of Naches and Tieton in Yakima County, within the Columbia Valley AVA. It sits on a lava flow about a million years old with andesite bedrock, rather than the basalt found across most of the Columbia Valley. Its first vinifera were planted in 2002.

**Climate.** At 1,200 to 2,100 feet and close to the Cascades, it is cooler and wetter than much of the Columbia Valley, with 10 to 13 inches of rain a year. The plateau's gentle grade lets cold air drain to lower ground, lowering the risk of frost.

**Soils.** Wholly above the level of the Missoula Floods, so the soils are wind-blown loess, still accumulating, with a good deal of clay that helps hold water.

**Grape varieties (text).** Pinot Gris and Riesling lead, with Gewürztraminer and Syrah.

**Wine styles (text).** Mostly white wines from Pinot Gris, Riesling and Gewürztraminer, and red wine from Syrah.

**Key facts**

- Established 2011 (27 CFR 9.222)
- About 13,254 acres (Washington State Wine Commission)
- Yakima County
- Every vineyard is farmed organically, biodynamically or Salmon-Safe, or a mix of these

## United States › Washington › Columbia Valley › Rocky Reach

Name: **Rocky Reach** · key `united-states.washington.columbia-valley.rocky-reach` · AVA

**Description.** An AVA of about 32,333 acres straddling the Columbia River between Wenatchee and Lake Chelan, in Chelan and Douglas counties, within the Columbia Valley AVA. The river and the Rocky Reach Reservoir cover about a quarter of it. Its name comes from steamboat captains of the late 1800s, who used it for this rocky stretch of rapids.

**Climate.** The low terraces in the river canyon are warmer, with a longer growing season, than the mountains and plateaus around them, which lets grapes ripen this far north. About 11 inches of rain falls a year.

**Soils.** Wind-blown sand and silt over cobblestones and gravel left by glacial outwash and floods, on crystalline bedrock rich in quartz and mica, which among Columbia Valley AVAs it shares only with Lake Chelan. The stony surface warms quickly and gives off heat to the vines.

**Grape varieties (text).** Cabernet Sauvignon is the leading grape on its roughly 117 acres of vineyards.

**Wine styles (text).** Mainly red wines, led by Cabernet Sauvignon.

**Key facts**

- Established 2022 (27 CFR 9.287)
- Chelan and Douglas counties
- 7 commercial vineyards on about 117 acres at approval (T.D. TTB-183)
- About 24% of the area is river and reservoir

## United States › Washington › Columbia Valley › Royal Slope

Name: **Royal Slope** · key `united-states.washington.columbia-valley.royal-slope` · AVA

**Description.** An AVA of about 156,389 acres in Grant and Adams counties: a broad, mostly south-facing slope around Royal City between the Quincy Basin and the Saddle Mountains, within the Columbia Valley AVA. It lies between the Ancient Lakes AVA to the north and the Wahluke Slope to the south. Wine grapes were first planted here in 1983.

**Climate.** Cooler than the Wahluke Slope about 15 miles south and warmer than Ancient Lakes, with 6 to 8 inches of rain a year. Elevations run from 610 feet to 1,756 feet on the Frenchman Hills ridge; higher sites ripen later and keep more acidity, and the slope lets cold air drain away.

**Soils.** Fine sand with silt and sandy loam, on a slope that is part of the Yakima Fold Belt.

**Grape varieties (text).** Cabernet Sauvignon leads, with Syrah and Chardonnay among a wide range of varieties.

**Wine styles (text).** Red wines led by Cabernet Sauvignon and Syrah, and white wines such as Chardonnay.

- Grant and Adams counties

## United States › Washington › Columbia Valley › The Burn of Columbia Valley

Name: **The Burn of Columbia Valley** · key `united-states.washington.columbia-valley.the-burn-of-columbia-valley` · AVA

**Description.** An AVA of about 16,870 acres in Klickitat County, on benches above the Columbia River west of the Horse Heaven Hills, within the Columbia Valley AVA. The name has been used for the area for more than 100 years, though its origin is unclear. Almost all of its vineyards have been planted since 2015.

**Climate.** It averages about 2,763 growing degree days, a little above the Columbia Valley's norm, but steady winds from the nearby Columbia Gorge slow the vines and keep canopies and berries small, so harvest comes late. It averages 8.76 inches of rain a year, more than much of the valley.

**Soils.** Mainly silty loams of the Mollisol order, largely Walla Walla silt loam, which hold more water and organic matter than the soils around them, on southeast-facing benches with an average slope of about 7%.

**Grape varieties (text).** Cabernet Sauvignon is the main grape, and Syrah the other most planted variety.

**Wine styles (text).** Red wines, chiefly from Cabernet Sauvignon and Syrah.

**Key facts**

- Established 2021 (27 CFR 9.276)
- Klickitat County
- 3 commercial vineyards on about 1,261 acres at approval (T.D. TTB-168)
- Precipitation of about 6.7 to 10.4 inches a year across the area (T.D. TTB-168)

## United States › Washington › Columbia Valley › Wahluke Slope

Name: **Wahluke Slope** · key `united-states.washington.columbia-valley.wahluke-slope` · AVA

**Description.** An AVA of about 81,000 acres near Mattawa in Grant County, bounded by the Columbia River, the Saddle Mountains and the Hanford Reach National Monument, within the Columbia Valley AVA. The whole AVA sits on one large ancestral alluvial fan with a steady grade of under 8%. It holds about 15% of Washington's vineyard acreage.

**Climate.** Among the warmest and driest parts of the state, with about 6 inches of rain a year. The slope and the nearby Columbia River lower the risk of frost; elevations run from 425 feet at the river to 1,480 feet, though most vineyards lie below 1,000 feet.

**Soils.** Deep wind-blown sand, more than 5 feet deep on average, which drains freely and makes vine vigor and ripening unusually even across the area.

**Grape varieties (text).** Mainly red grapes, above all Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot and Syrah, with Chardonnay.

**Wine styles (text).** Red wines from Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot and Syrah, and some white wine from Chardonnay.

**Key facts**

- Grant County
- About 8,932 acres under vine (Washington State Wine Commission)

## United States › Washington › Columbia Valley › Walla Walla Valley

Name: **Walla Walla Valley** · key `united-states.washington.columbia-valley.walla-walla-valley` · AVA

**Description.** An AVA of about 300,000 acres around the town of Walla Walla that spans Walla Walla County, Washington, and Umatilla County, Oregon; TTB lists both states. It lies within the Columbia Valley AVA and holds The Rocks District of Milton-Freewater, on the Oregon side. It has the highest concentration of wineries in Washington, including some of the state's oldest.

**Climate.** Cooler and wetter than many Washington AVAs. Rainfall rises from about 9 inches a year in the west to 22 inches at the eastern edge in the foothills of the Blue Mountains, where a few vineyards can be farmed without irrigation.

**Soils.** Mostly well-drained sandy loess over Missoula Flood slackwater deposits and fractured basalt; above 1,200 feet, the floods' high-water mark, the loess lies directly on basalt. In the south an old riverbed of cobblestones defines The Rocks District.

**Grape varieties (text).** Mostly red grapes, led by Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot and Syrah.

**Wine styles (text).** Mainly red wines from Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot and Syrah.

**Key facts**

- Established 1984 (27 CFR 9.91)
- About 2,933 acres under vine, 1,672 of them in Washington (Washington State Wine Commission)
- More than 120 wineries (Washington State Wine Commission)
- Its name comes from a Native American term meaning 'many waters'

## United States › Washington › Columbia Valley › White Bluffs

Name: **White Bluffs** · key `united-states.washington.columbia-valley.white-bluffs` · AVA

**Description.** An AVA of about 93,738 acres in Franklin County, on a plateau north of Pasco that rises about 200 feet above the land around it, within the Columbia Valley AVA. It is named for the whitish lake-bed sediments of the Ringold Formation, exposed in bluffs along the Columbia River. Its first vines were planted in 1972.

**Climate.** The plateau's height lengthens the growing season by about 45 days on average compared with the surrounding area and helps protect vines from frost and freezes. About 6 inches of rain falls a year.

**Soils.** Wind-deposited silt and fine sand over ice-age flood sediment, on the clay-rich lake-bed sediments of the Ringold Formation and a thick layer of caliche, so vine roots never reach basalt.

**Grape varieties (text).** Cabernet Sauvignon and Merlot lead, with Syrah, Riesling and Sauvignon Blanc.

## United States › Washington › Columbia Valley › Yakima Valley

Name: **Yakima Valley** · key `united-states.washington.columbia-valley.yakima-valley` · AVA

**Description.** Washington's first AVA, established in 1983 in Benton and Yakima counties within the Columbia Valley AVA, and the first in the Pacific Northwest. The Yakima River runs through it, and it holds nearly a third of the state's planted vinifera acreage. It contains the Red Mountain, Rattlesnake Hills, Snipes Mountain, Goose Gap and Candy Mountain AVAs.

**Climate.** Cooler toward the Cascade foothills in the west and hot toward the center of the Columbia Basin, with about 8 inches of rain a year; the Yakima River and local aquifers supply water for irrigation.

**Soils.** Moderate to deep silt loam laid down by the Missoula Floods, over gravel or directly on basalt bedrock, well drained for irrigated vines.

**Grape varieties (text).** One of the few Washington AVAs planted more to white grapes than red, led by Chardonnay and Riesling; its warmer sites grow Merlot, Cabernet Sauvignon and Syrah.

**Wine styles (text).** White wines from Chardonnay and Riesling, and red wines from Merlot, Cabernet Sauvignon and Syrah.

- About 18,924 acres under vine (Washington State Wine Commission)

**Climate.** On north-facing slopes the grapes receive less sun than on the south- and southwest-facing sites nearby and usually ripen later than the same varieties on Red Mountain. About 6 inches of rain falls a year.

## United States › Washington › Columbia Valley › Yakima Valley › Rattlesnake Hills

Name: **Rattlesnake Hills** · key `united-states.washington.columbia-valley.yakima-valley.rattlesnake-hills` · AVA

**Description.** An AVA of about 68,500 acres on the south slope of the Rattlesnake Hills around Zillah in Yakima County, within the Yakima Valley AVA. The hills are an east-west fold of the Yakima Fold Belt and take their name from nearby Rattlesnake Mountain. The earliest vines here were planted in 1968.

**Climate.** Generally higher than the Yakima Valley as a whole, from 850 to 3,085 feet, with vineyards on the lower ground; the height lessens the risk of spring and fall frost, and winters are milder than in the land around it. Rainfall is 6 to 12 inches a year.

**Soils.** Mostly silt loam and loam. About half the AVA is underlain by Ellensburg Formation sediments eroded from the Cascade volcanoes, and the higher vineyards lie above the reach of the Missoula Floods.

**Description.** An AVA of about 4,040 acres near Benton City in Benton County, within the Yakima Valley AVA. ## United States › Washington › Columbia Valley › Yakima Valley › Snipes Mountain

Name: **Snipes Mountain** · key `united-states.washington.columbia-valley.yakima-valley.snipes-mountain` · AVA

**Description.** An AVA of about 4,145 acres on Snipes Mountain, a fold of the Yakima Fold Belt between Sunnyside and Granger in Yakima County, within the Yakima Valley AVA. It is named after cattle rancher Ben Snipes, who built a home at its base in the 1800s. Vineyards were first planted here in 1914, and Harrison Hill holds Cabernet Sauvignon vines from 1963.

**Climate.** Steep north- and south-facing slopes let cold air drain downhill, helping to prevent the frost damage that sometimes hits nearby areas. About 7 inches of rain falls a year.

**Soils.** Loess over Missoula Flood sediment and, in many places, fist- to melon-sized cobbles and gravel laid down by an ancestral course of the Columbia River; only the top 90 feet of the mountain rise above the floods.

**Grape varieties (text).** Chardonnay, Cabernet Sauvignon and Merlot lead the plantings.

**Wine styles (text).** Red wines from Cabernet Sauvignon and Merlot, and white wine from Chardonnay.

**Key facts**

- Established 2009 (27 CFR 9.213)
- Yakima County
- About 859 acres under vine (Washington State Wine Commission)
- Its boundary follows the 750-foot and 820-foot elevation contours (27 CFR 9.213)
