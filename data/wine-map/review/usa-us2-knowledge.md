## United States

Name: **United States** · key `united-states` · country

**Description.** Wine is made across the United States, but California alone makes about 81 per cent of it. This map covers the four states that matter most for fine wine: California, Washington, Oregon and New York. Wine regions here are American Viticultural Areas (AVAs): grape-growing areas the federal Alcohol and Tobacco Tax and Trade Bureau (TTB) defines by geography, with no rules on grapes, yields or winemaking.

**Climate.** Too varied for one description. The West Coast has dry summers: the cold Pacific and coastal fog cool California's coastal valleys, and the Cascade Mountains leave eastern Washington and Oregon in a dry rain shadow. New York's vineyards rely on deep lakes and the Atlantic to soften cold continental winters.

**Soils.** As varied as the climate: volcanic, marine sedimentary and wind-blown loess soils in Oregon's Willamette Valley; loess over Ice Age flood gravels and basalt in Washington's Columbia Valley; decomposed granite in California's Sierra Foothills; shale and glacial deposits in New York's Finger Lakes; sandy glacial soils on Long Island.

**Grape varieties (text).** International varieties lead: Cabernet Sauvignon and Chardonnay in California and Washington, Pinot Noir in Oregon, Riesling in the Finger Lakes. Zinfandel has been grown in California since the Gold Rush.

**Wine styles (text).** Mostly dry reds and whites, usually labelled by grape variety rather than by place. Rosé, sparkling wine, sweet wines and a few fortified wines are made too.

**Key facts**

- TTB lists 280 established AVAs (August 2026); California has 154 of them
- An AVA name on the label means at least 85% of the grapes come from that AVA (27 CFR 4.25)
- A state or county name needs 75% of the wine from that place; a grape name needs 75% of that grape (27 CFR 4.23)
- The first AVA was Augusta, Missouri, established on 20 June 1980
- California makes about 81% of US wine (Wine Institute)

## United States › California

Name: **California** · key `united-states.california` · region

**Description.** The largest wine state by far: about 81 per cent of the country's wine, from roughly 540,000 acres of wine grapes. Its AVAs run the length of the state; this map groups them under the North Coast, Central Coast, Sierra Foothills and South Coast AVAs and the Central Valley. Napa Valley, designated in 1981, was California's first AVA.

**Climate.** Mediterranean: mild, wet winters and long, dry summers. Near the coast, marine air and fog from the cold Pacific move inland through gaps and valleys in the Coast Ranges, so the coolest vineyards lie closest to the ocean and the warmest in the inland valleys and the foothills.

**Soils.** Every kind, changing over short distances: alluvial valley floors, volcanic soils in Napa and Lake County, calcareous soils in Paso Robles, decomposed granite in the Sierra Foothills and Temecula Valley, and deep sandy loam washed down from the Sierra Nevada around Lodi.

**Grape varieties (text).** Cabernet Sauvignon and Chardonnay are the two most planted wine grapes. Pinot Noir comes from the cooler coast, Zinfandel from Lodi, Sonoma and the Sierra Foothills, and Petite Sirah (Durif) is widely grown for deep-coloured reds.

**Wine styles (text).** Mostly dry varietal reds and whites, from cool-coast Pinot Noir and Chardonnay to ripe Cabernet Sauvignon and Zinfandel. Sparkling wine and rosé are made too, and sweet and fortified wines in the Central Valley.

**Key facts**

- About 540,000 acres of wine grapes (CDFA estimate for 2025)
- 154 of the 280 US AVAs lie wholly or partly in California (TTB)
- Wine labelled California must be made only from California grapes, stricter than the federal 75% (17 CCR 17015)
- At the 1976 Paris tasting, a 1973 Stag's Leap Cabernet Sauvignon and a 1973 Chateau Montelena Chardonnay placed first
- Napa Valley, designated in 1981, was California's first AVA

## United States › California › Central Coast

Name: **Central Coast** · key `united-states.california.central-coast` · AVA

**Description.** A long umbrella AVA, established in 1985, that runs from the San Francisco Bay area south to Santa Barbara County. It takes in, among others, the Santa Cruz Mountains, Livermore Valley, the Monterey AVAs, Paso Robles and its eleven districts, Edna Valley, and the Santa Barbara AVAs from Santa Maria Valley to Sta. Rita Hills.

**Climate.** Cooled by the Pacific along its whole length. Marine air from Monterey Bay gives Monterey County one of the longest growing seasons in California. In Santa Barbara County the mountains run east to west, so the valleys open straight onto the ocean and draw fog and breezes inland; Paso Robles, further from the sea, has wide swings between day and night.

**Soils.** Mixed: granite, marine sediments and volcanic rock. Paso Robles stands out for calcareous soils with a high pH of 7.4 to 8.6, and calcareous shale on its western hills.

**Grape varieties (text).** Chardonnay and Pinot Noir in the cool western valleys of Monterey and Santa Barbara; Cabernet Sauvignon, Syrah, Zinfandel and Grenache in warmer Paso Robles and the eastern Santa Barbara valleys.

**Wine styles (text).** Dry reds and whites, from cool-climate Pinot Noir and Chardonnay to Rhône and Bordeaux varieties inland; rosé and sparkling wine too.

**Key facts**

- Established 1985 (27 CFR 9.75)
- Covers parts of ten counties, from San Francisco and Alameda south to Santa Barbara
- Paso Robles has eleven nested AVAs, all established in 2014 (TTB)
- Vines were first planted in Livermore Valley in the 1840s; Wente and Concannon planted theirs in the early 1880s
- Santa Barbara County's transverse valleys run east to west, open to Pacific fog

## United States › California › Central Valley

Name: **Central Valley** · key `united-states.california.central-valley` · a grouping on this map, not an AVA

**Description.** Central Valley is a grouping on this map, not an AVA. It gathers the eleven AVAs of the Sacramento and San Joaquin valleys that sit in no larger umbrella AVA: Lodi, Clarksburg, Madera, Capay Valley, Diablo Grande, Dunnigan Hills, Paulsell Valley, River Junction, Salado Creek, Tracy Hills and Winters Highlands. Lodi lies where the two valleys meet, and grapes came to it with the Gold Rush.

**Climate.** Hot, dry summers. Around Lodi and Clarksburg, breezes from San Francisco Bay come up through the Carquinez Strait and the Sacramento-San Joaquin Delta and cool the vineyards in the evening; further south, around Madera, the valley is warmer, which suits the sweet and fortified wines made there.

**Soils.** Around Lodi, deep, well-drained sandy loams of granite washed down from the Sierra Nevada by the Mokelumne River; elsewhere the valley floor is alluvial.

**Grape varieties (text).** Zinfandel above all around Lodi, much of it from old bush vines, with Cabernet Sauvignon, Chardonnay and Petite Sirah. Clarksburg is known for Chenin Blanc, and Madera for Muscat.

**Wine styles (text).** Dry reds and whites, led by Lodi Zinfandel, plus sweet Muscat and port-style fortified wines from Madera.

**Key facts**

- A navigation grouping on this map, with eleven member AVAs; it has no legal status
- Lodi has thousands of acres of old bush vines, most of them Zinfandel, many dry-farmed and own-rooted
- Clarksburg, in the Sacramento River Delta, is known for Chenin Blanc
- In Madera, Ficklin Vineyards has made port-style wine since 1948 and Quady Winery makes sweet Muscat

## United States › California › North Coast

Name: **North Coast** · key `united-states.california.north-coast` · AVA

**Description.** The umbrella AVA for California's northern coastal counties, established in 1983. It covers parts of Lake, Marin, Mendocino, Napa, Solano and Sonoma counties and contains, among many others, Napa Valley, Russian River Valley, Sonoma Coast and Anderson Valley. More than half of California's wineries are in this region.

**Climate.** Mediterranean, and cooler toward the ocean. Marine air and fog come in from the Pacific and San Francisco Bay, so the Sonoma coast and the Carneros district by the bay are cool, while valleys and ridges further inland are warmer.

**Soils.** Varied over short distances: alluvial valley floors, volcanic soils on the ridges, and red volcanic soils around Mount Konocti, a dormant volcano, in Lake County.

**Grape varieties (text).** Cabernet Sauvignon above all in Napa Valley; Chardonnay and Pinot Noir in the cooler districts near the coast and the bay; Zinfandel in Sonoma and Mendocino.

**Wine styles (text).** Dry reds and whites, from Napa Cabernet Sauvignon to coastal Pinot Noir and Chardonnay, and sparkling wine from the cooler districts.

**Key facts**

- Established 1983 (27 CFR 9.30)
- Covers parts of Lake, Marin, Mendocino, Napa, Solano and Sonoma counties
- Contains Napa Valley, California's first AVA (1981), which grows about 4% of the state's wine grapes
- Home to more than half of California's wineries (Discover California Wines)

## United States › California › Sierra Foothills

Name: **Sierra Foothills** · key `united-states.california.sierra-foothills` · AVA

**Description.** A long AVA on the western slopes of the Sierra Nevada, established in 1987 across eight counties from Yuba in the north to Mariposa in the south. Vines came with the Gold Rush, and some of the oldest Zinfandel vineyards still in production are here. It contains El Dorado, Fair Play, California Shenandoah Valley, Fiddletown and North Yuba.

**Climate.** Warm, dry summers, tempered by height: nearly all its Zinfandel vineyards sit between about 1,200 and 3,500 feet, where nights are cooler than on the valley floor below.

**Soils.** Largely decomposed granite on the foothill slopes, well drained, with volcanic soils in parts of Amador and El Dorado counties.

**Grape varieties (text).** Zinfandel, about 40 per cent of the vineyard acreage, much of it from old vines; Barbera, Syrah and Petite Sirah are the other main reds.

**Wine styles (text).** Mostly dry reds, above all Zinfandel, with some whites.

**Key facts**

- Established 1987 (27 CFR 9.120)
- Eight counties: Yuba, Nevada, Placer, El Dorado, Amador, Calaveras, Tuolumne and Mariposa
- Zinfandel is about 40% of the vineyard acreage (ZAP)
- The Original Grandpere Vineyard, planted in 1869, is the oldest documented Zinfandel vineyard still producing

## United States › California › South Coast

Name: **South Coast** · key `united-states.california.south-coast` · AVA

**Description.** The umbrella AVA for Southern California's vineyards, established in 1985 in parts of Orange, Riverside and San Diego counties. Its main wine district is Temecula Valley, inland in Riverside County; Ramona Valley, San Luis Rey and San Pasqual Valley are the other AVAs inside it.

**Climate.** Warm and sunny, cooled where the coastal mountains open. In Temecula Valley, hot air rising over the desert draws Pacific air in through the Rainbow Gap, so warm days are followed by cool nights.

**Soils.** Temecula Valley's vineyards are largely on well-drained decomposed granite.

**Grape varieties (text).** Cabernet Sauvignon, Syrah, Chardonnay and Sauvignon Blanc, among some thirty varieties grown in Temecula Valley, including Sangiovese, Vermentino and Barbera.

**Wine styles (text).** Dry reds, whites and rosé, much of it sold at the wineries themselves.

**Key facts**

- Established 1985 (27 CFR 9.104)
- Covers parts of Orange, Riverside and San Diego counties
- Temecula Valley sits between the Pacific and the desert, about an hour north-east of San Diego
- Contains the Temecula Valley, Ramona Valley, San Luis Rey and San Pasqual Valley AVAs

## United States › New York

Name: **New York** · key `united-states.new-york` · region

**Description.** Most of New York's vineyards grow native and hybrid grapes, above all Concord, for juice and jam: Concord makes up about 80 per cent of the state's grape tonnage. Its fine wine comes from European vinifera grapes, planted with success only from the 1950s, chiefly in the Finger Lakes and on Long Island. The state has eleven AVAs, including Lake Erie and the Hudson River Region.

**Climate.** Continental, with cold winters once thought too harsh for European vines. The wine regions sit beside water: deep lakes store summer heat and delay spring frost in the Finger Lakes, and the Atlantic and Long Island Sound keep Long Island's winters milder than the rest of the state.

**Soils.** Glacial in origin almost everywhere: grey shale and silty limestone bedrock under varied glacial soils in the Finger Lakes, and sandy, well-drained glacial moraine on Long Island.

**Grape varieties (text).** Riesling leads the vinifera in the Finger Lakes, with Chardonnay, Cabernet Franc, Pinot Noir and Gewürztraminer; Merlot and Cabernet Franc lead on Long Island. Concord, Niagara and hybrids such as Seyval Blanc cover much of the rest of the state.

**Wine styles (text).** Dry to sweet Riesling and other aromatic whites, lighter-bodied reds, rosé and traditional-method sparkling wine.

**Key facts**

- Eleven AVAs lie wholly or partly in New York (TTB)
- Concord is about 80% of New York's grape tonnage, grown mostly for juice and jam (Cornell, 2018)
- The Lake Erie region is the largest and oldest Concord-growing area in the world
- Konstantin Frank planted vinifera on Keuka Lake from 1958 and released his first wines in 1962
- Long Island's first vineyard was planted in 1973

## United States › New York › Finger Lakes

Name: **Finger Lakes** · key `united-states.new-york.finger-lakes` · AVA

**Description.** An AVA in upstate New York, established in 1982, around a series of long, narrow glacial lakes, with Canandaigua, Keuka, Seneca and Cayuga at the heart of its wine growing. It has about 10,700 acres of vineyard and 144 wineries, and is known above all for Riesling. Seneca Lake and Cayuga Lake are AVAs within it.

**Climate.** Cool and continental. The lakes, some of the deepest in the United States, store heat from March to August and give it back for the rest of the year, which delays spring frost and stretches the growing season to about 195 days.

**Soils.** Devonian bedrock of grey shale and silty limestone, or of shale, siltstone, sandstone and limestone together, under glacial soils that change from site to site.

**Grape varieties (text).** Riesling first, then Cabernet Franc, Pinot Noir, Gewürztraminer and Chardonnay; Lemberger (Blaufränkisch) is a local red. Native and hybrid grapes are still widely grown.

**Wine styles (text).** Riesling from dry to sweet, other aromatic whites, light reds, rosé and sparkling wine.

**Key facts**

- Established 1982 (27 CFR 9.34)
- About 10,700 acres of vineyard and 144 wineries (NYWGF)
- Contains the Cayuga Lake (1988) and Seneca Lake (2003) AVAs
- Growing season about 195 days, lengthened by the lakes
- Konstantin Frank's vinifera plantings on Keuka Lake began in 1958

## United States › New York › Long Island

Name: **Long Island** · key `united-states.new-york.long-island` · AVA

**Description.** The Long Island AVA, established in 2001, covers Nassau and Suffolk counties, and its vineyards lie at the eastern end, on the North Fork and the South Fork. Alex and Louisa Hargrave planted the first vineyard at Cutchogue in 1973; the island now has about 1,815 acres of vines. It contains the North Fork of Long Island and The Hamptons, Long Island AVAs, both older than it.

**Climate.** Maritime. The Atlantic, Long Island Sound and Peconic Bay moderate the temperature: long, warm summers, mild springs and autumns, winters much milder than upstate, and a growing season of about 225 days.

**Soils.** Glacial and sandy: deep, well-drained soils on the northern moraine, and coarser, fast-draining soils on the southern one.

**Grape varieties (text).** Merlot and Cabernet Franc lead the reds, with Cabernet Sauvignon; Chardonnay and Sauvignon Blanc lead the whites.

**Wine styles (text).** Medium-bodied reds from Bordeaux varieties, crisp whites, rosé and sparkling wine.

**Key facts**

- Established 2001 (27 CFR 9.170); The Hamptons (1985) and North Fork (1986) AVAs came first
- First vineyard planted in 1973, at Cutchogue on the North Fork
- About 1,815 acres of vineyard and 82 wineries (NYWGF)
- Growing season about 225 days

## United States › Oregon › Willamette Valley

Name: **Willamette Valley** · key `united-states.oregon.willamette-valley` · AVA

**Description.** Oregon's main wine region, about 150 miles long from Portland south to Eugene, and an AVA since 1983. It has 25,450 acres under vine, more than 700 wineries and eleven nested AVAs, from the Dundee Hills and Eola-Amity Hills to Mount Pisgah, Polk County. Pinot Noir is its grape.

**Climate.** Cool, with cool, wet winters and warm, dry summers, and warm days and cool nights that keep acidity in the grapes. The Coast Range to the west and the Cascades to the east shelter the valley; the Van Duzer Corridor, a gap in the Coast Range, lets afternoon ocean winds into its western side.

**Soils.** Three main types on the hillsides: red volcanic Jory soils formed from Columbia River basalts, as in the Dundee Hills; marine sedimentary soils such as Willakenzie, as in Yamhill-Carlton; and wind-blown Laurelwood loess in the Chehalem Mountains. Most vineyards sit above about 200 feet, clear of the Missoula flood soils on the valley floor.

**Grape varieties (text).** Pinot Noir is about 70 per cent of the valley's grapes; Pinot Gris, Chardonnay and Riesling are the main others, with smaller plantings such as Pinot Blanc.

**Wine styles (text).** Dry Pinot Noir above all, with Pinot Gris and Chardonnay whites, and rosé and sparkling wine from the same grapes.

**Key facts**

- Established 1983 (27 CFR 9.90)
- 25,450 acres under vine (Oregon Wine Board)
- Eleven nested AVAs, the newest Mount Pisgah, Polk County, Oregon (2022)
- David Lett planted the first Pinot Noir in the Dundee Hills in 1966, at The Eyrie Vineyards
- Lett's 1975 Pinot Noir placed highly at the 1979 Gault-Millau Wine Olympiad in Paris

## United States › Washington

Name: **Washington** · key `united-states.washington` · region

**Description.** The second-largest wine-producing state, with about 35,000 acres of wine grapes and close to 1,000 wineries. Almost all of it lies east of the Cascade Mountains: over 99 per cent of the state's vineyard acreage is in the Columbia Valley AVA. Washington has 22 AVAs; Columbia Valley, Columbia Gorge and Walla Walla Valley are shared with Oregon, and Lewis-Clark Valley with Idaho.

**Climate.** East of the Cascades the vineyards sit in a rain shadow, with 6 to 8 inches of rain a year in the Columbia Valley, and are irrigated from the Columbia River and its tributaries. Summer days bring up to 17 hours of sunlight, and days and nights differ by 30 to 40°F. West of the Cascades, Puget Sound is cool and wet.

**Soils.** Wind-blown loess and sand over gravel and slackwater sediment left by the Ice Age floods, with basalt beneath. Most vines grow on their own roots rather than grafted rootstock, since phylloxera survives poorly in very sandy soils.

**Grape varieties (text).** Cabernet Sauvignon, Chardonnay, Merlot, Riesling and Syrah lead among more than 80 varieties grown, with Cabernet Franc and Sauvignon Blanc behind them.

**Wine styles (text).** Mostly dry reds and whites, the reds led by Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot and Syrah; Riesling is made from dry to sweet, and some rosé.

**Key facts**

- The second-largest wine-producing state in the US (Washington State Wine Commission)
- About 35,000 acres of wine grapes, 985 licensed wineries and 22 AVAs
- Over 99% of the state's vineyard acreage is in the Columbia Valley AVA

## United States › Washington › Puget Sound

## New grapes for the catalog

- **Petite Sirah** (red; skin blue-black): The name California uses for Durif, a French variety. DNA work at UC Davis (Meredith and others, 1999) found that 49 of 53 vines sold as Petite Sirah in California were Durif, and that Durif is most probably a seedling of Peloursin with Syrah as its other parent.
