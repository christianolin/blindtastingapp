## United States › California › North Coast › Napa Valley › Oak Knoll District of Napa Valley

Name: **Oak Knoll District of Napa Valley** · key `united-states.california.north-coast.napa-valley.oak-knoll-district-of-napa-valley` · AVA

**Description.** A flat stretch of the valley floor at the southern end of central Napa Valley, just north of the city of Napa. Joseph Osborne planted vinifera vines here in 1851. The legal name ends in "of Napa Valley", so it already carries the valley's name.

**Climate.** Cool and even: daily fog from San Pablo Bay and afternoon breezes give one of the valley's coolest sub-AVAs and a long growing season; mid-summer highs reach about 92 °F and nights fall to about 50 °F.

**Soils.** River-laid soils in the centre, gravelly alluvial fans on the west side below Mt. Veeder, and silty clay loams toward the river in the south and east.

**Grape varieties (text).** Cabernet Sauvignon, Chardonnay and Merlot, with Sauvignon Blanc and Riesling.

**Wine styles (text).** Dry red wines from Cabernet Sauvignon and Merlot, and dry whites from Chardonnay, Sauvignon Blanc and Riesling.

**Key facts**

- Established 2004 (27 CFR 9.161)
- Over 4,150 acres planted (Napa Valley Vintners)
- Elevations from sea level to about 800 feet (Napa Valley Vintners)
- Growing season averages about eight months (Napa Valley Vintners)

## United States › California › North Coast › Napa Valley › Oakville

Name: **Oakville** · key `united-states.california.north-coast.napa-valley.oakville` · AVA

**Description.** A valley-floor AVA in the middle of Napa Valley, running from the Mayacamas foothills on the west to the Vaca foothills on the east, between Yountville and Rutherford. About three quarters of its vineyard is Cabernet Sauvignon. Wines labelled Oakville must also carry the Napa Valley name.

**Climate.** Moderately warm, with summer days commonly in the mid-90s °F; evening and early-morning fog keeps acidity in the grapes, and the eastern side gets more afternoon sun.

**Soils.** Gravelly, sedimentary alluvial loams on the western side and heavier volcanic soils on the eastern side; fairly deep and well drained.

**Grape varieties (text).** Cabernet Sauvignon above all, with Merlot, Cabernet Franc and Sauvignon Blanc.

**Wine styles (text).** Dry red wines, mainly Cabernet Sauvignon and Bordeaux-style blends, and dry Sauvignon Blanc.

**Key facts**

- Established 1993 (27 CFR 9.134)
- Over 5,000 acres planted; about 75% Cabernet Sauvignon (Napa Valley Vintners)

## United States › California › North Coast › Napa Valley › Rutherford

Name: **Rutherford** · key `united-states.california.north-coast.napa-valley.rutherford` · AVA

**Description.** A valley-floor AVA at the widest point of Napa Valley, between Oakville and St. Helena, from the Mayacamas benchland on the west to the Vaca foothills on the east. Georges de Latour's Beaulieu winery made its name here after Prohibition. Wines labelled Rutherford must also carry the Napa Valley name.

**Climate.** Moderately warm, with summer peaks in the mid-90s °F, early-morning fog and a wide difference between day and night; the western bench is cooled by afternoon marine wind.

**Soils.** Sedimentary, gravelly and sandy alluvial soils on the western benchland and moderately deep volcanic soils on the eastern side; locals speak of "Rutherford dust" for the fine-grained tannin of the bench wines.

**Grape varieties (text).** Cabernet Sauvignon dominates, with Merlot, Cabernet Franc and Sauvignon Blanc.

**Wine styles (text).** Dry red wines, mostly Cabernet Sauvignon and Bordeaux-style blends, and dry Sauvignon Blanc.

**Key facts**

- Elevations from about 155 to 500 feet (Napa Valley Vintners)
- Rainfall up to about 38 inches a year (Napa Valley Vintners)

## United States › California › North Coast › Napa Valley › Spring Mountain District

Name: **Spring Mountain District** · key `united-states.california.north-coast.napa-valley.spring-mountain-district` · AVA

**Description.** A mountain AVA on the eastern slopes of the Mayacamas range directly west of St. Helena, with small vineyards cut into forest on steep, mostly east-facing ground. Charles Lemme is the first documented grape grower here, in 1874. Wines labelled Spring Mountain District must also carry the Napa Valley name.

**Climate.** Cool to moderate depending on height and aspect: warmer nights and cooler days than the valley floor, and the coolest, wettest of the Napa sub-AVAs.

**Soils.** Mostly sedimentary: weathered sandstone and shale, loamy and friable, well drained and low in fertility, over Franciscan mélange in the south; volcanic soils toward the north end.

**Grape varieties (text).** Cabernet Sauvignon first, with Merlot, Cabernet Franc, Petite Sirah, Chardonnay and Riesling.

**Wine styles (text).** Dry red wines, mainly Cabernet Sauvignon, and some dry Chardonnay and Riesling.

**Key facts**

- Established 1993 (27 CFR 9.143)
- Elevations from about 600 to 2,600 feet (Napa Valley Vintners)
- About 30 miles from the Pacific (Napa Valley Vintners)
- Labels must also show "Napa Valley" (Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code §25240)

## United States › California › North Coast › Napa Valley › St. Helena

Name: **St. Helena** · key `united-states.california.north-coast.napa-valley.st-helena` · AVA

**Description.** An hourglass-shaped AVA around the town of St. Helena, taking in the narrowest part of Napa Valley between Rutherford and Calistoga. Charles Krug founded his winery here in 1861. Wines labelled St. Helena must also carry the Napa Valley name.

**Climate.** One of the warmer parts of the Napa Valley floor, at times hotter than Calistoga, with rainfall of about 40 inches a year.

## United States › California › North Coast › Northern Sonoma

Name: **Northern Sonoma** · key `united-states.california.north-coast.northern-sonoma` · AVA

**Description.** A large umbrella AVA over northern Sonoma County that takes in Alexander Valley, Dry Creek Valley, Knights Valley, Russian River Valley and its sub-AVAs, from valley floors to mountain peaks. It was petitioned by E. & J. Gallo so that its Sonoma wines from vineyards in different valleys could carry one appellation, and few labels use it today.

**Climate.** Varied: cool and foggy toward the Russian River Valley and the coast, warmer in the inland valleys of Alexander and Knights Valley.

**Soils.** Varied, from river gravels and alluvium on the valley floors to volcanic and sedimentary soils in the hills.

**Grape varieties (text).** Cabernet Sauvignon, Chardonnay and Zinfandel, with Sauvignon Blanc, Pinot Noir and Merlot.

**Wine styles (text).** Dry red and white wines, from Cabernet Sauvignon and Zinfandel to Chardonnay.

**Key facts**

Name: **Dry Creek Valley** · key `united-states.california.north-coast.northern-sonoma.dry-creek-valley` · AVA

**Description.** A narrow valley about 16 miles long and 2 miles wide northwest of Healdsburg, running from the Russian River up to Lake Sonoma. It is known above all for Zinfandel, including old vines that survived Prohibition when growers switched to prunes, and for Sauvignon Blanc. Over 70 wineries, mostly family-owned, work here.

## United States › California › North Coast › Northern Sonoma › Russian River Valley

Name: **Russian River Valley** · key `united-states.california.north-coast.northern-sonoma.russian-river-valley` · AVA

**Description.** A cool AVA at the centre of Sonoma County, on the lower Russian River and the Santa Rosa Plain, from Healdsburg south toward Sebastopol and Santa Rosa. It does not follow the river all the way to the sea. Chardonnay and Pinot Noir are its main grapes, then Zinfandel. Most of it also lies inside the Sonoma Coast AVA, which overlaps it.

**Climate.** Warm days tempered by Pacific fog that lingers in the hills west of Sebastopol into the late afternoon; temperatures can drop 35 to 40 °F from day to night, and the growing season runs long.

**Soils.** Varied; the fine-grained Goldridge sandy loam, well drained and moderate in vigour, dominates its western Green Valley corner.

**Grape varieties (text).** Chardonnay, Pinot Noir and Zinfandel lead by acreage, with Sauvignon Blanc, Syrah, Merlot and Pinot Gris.

**Wine styles (text).** Dry Chardonnay and Pinot Noir, Zinfandel, and sparkling wine from the cooler western parts.

- Over 70 wineries (Sonoma County Tourism)

## United States › California › North Coast › Northern Sonoma › Russian River Valley › Chalk Hill

Name: **Chalk Hill** · key `united-states.california.north-coast.northern-sonoma.russian-river-valley.chalk-hill` · AVA

## United States › California › South Coast › Temecula Valley

Name: **Temecula Valley** · key `united-states.california.south-coast.temecula-valley` · AVA

**Description.** An inland valley in southwestern Riverside County, with vineyards at about 1,400 to 1,600 feet between the coastal mountains and Mount San Jacinto. Cool Pacific air reaches it each afternoon through the Rainbow Gap and the Santa Margarita Gap in the coastal mountains. It was called Temecula until 2004.

**Climate.** Warm days and cool nights: around late morning, heat over the desert to the east draws cool ocean air through the gaps, and the day-to-night swing can reach 40 to 50 °F.

**Soils.** Decomposed granite, rocky and sandy loams.

**Grape varieties (text).** Cabernet Sauvignon is the most planted, with Syrah, Chardonnay, Merlot, and Mediterranean varieties such as Sangiovese, Tempranillo and Viognier.

**Wine styles (text).** Dry reds from Bordeaux and Mediterranean varieties, dry whites and rosé.

**Key facts**

- Established 1984 (27 CFR 9.50)
- Named Temecula until June 2004 (27 CFR 9.50)
- Its boundary uses the 1,400- and 1,500-foot contour lines in places (27 CFR 9.50)
