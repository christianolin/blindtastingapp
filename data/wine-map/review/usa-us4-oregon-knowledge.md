## United States › Oregon › Columbia Gorge

Name: **Columbia Gorge** · key `united-states.oregon.columbia-gorge` · AVA

**Description.** An AVA about 60 miles east of Portland that lies on both banks of the Columbia River, in Oregon and Washington: Hood River and Wasco counties, Oregon, and Skamania and Klickitat counties, Washington. The river cuts the only sea-level passage through the Cascade Range, and over a short distance the climate shifts from wet and marine-influenced in the west to dry high desert in the east.

**Climate.** Rainfall drops from about 36 inches a year near the Cascades to about 10 inches in the east, and winds funnelled through the gorge, often 10 to 20 mph or more, cool and dry the vineyards. Vineyards range from near sea level to about 2,000 feet.

**Soils.** Volcanic soils, lahar deposits, loess, silt and sand; the final rule names silty loams of the Chemawa, Underwood and McGowen series, on benchlands left by ancient lava flows.

**Grape varieties (text).** Many varieties: Pinot Noir, Chardonnay, Gewürztraminer and Riesling in the cooler, wetter west, and Zinfandel and Cabernet Sauvignon in the drier east.

**Wine styles (text).** White wines from Chardonnay, Gewürztraminer and Riesling, and red wines from Pinot Noir in the west and from Zinfandel and Cabernet Sauvignon in the east.

**Key facts**

- Established 2004 (27 CFR 9.178)
- Hood River and Wasco counties (Oregon); Skamania and Klickitat counties (Washington)
- About 186,610 acres, 120,010 of them in Oregon (Oregon Wine Board)
- About 925 acres planted (Oregon Wine Board)

## United States › Oregon › Southern Oregon › Rogue Valley

Name: **Rogue Valley** · key `united-states.oregon.southern-oregon.rogue-valley` · AVA

**Description.** A large AVA in Jackson and Josephine counties, within the Southern Oregon AVA, made up of three distinct valleys with progressively warmer climates along the Rogue River and its tributaries. It lies about 75 miles from the coast, framed by the Cascade, Siskiyou and Coast ranges. Peter Britt began growing grapes here around 1852 and in 1873 founded Valley View, Oregon's first official winery.

**Climate.** Warm, dry summers, with nights much cooler than the days, which keeps acidity in the grapes and lengthens the ripening season.

**Soils.** Mainly stream sediments laid down by the Rogue River and its tributaries, varying from one of its three valleys to the next.

**Grape varieties (text).** Cabernet Sauvignon, Syrah and Viognier lead, with Pinot Noir, Chardonnay and Pinot Gris.

**Wine styles (text).** Red wines from Cabernet Sauvignon, Syrah and Pinot Noir, and white wines from Viognier, Chardonnay and Pinot Gris.

**Key facts**

- Established 1991 (27 CFR 9.132)
- Jackson and Josephine counties
- About 5,635 acres planted (Oregon Wine Board)
- Contains the Applegate Valley AVA

## United States › Oregon › Southern Oregon › Rogue Valley › Applegate Valley

Name: **Applegate Valley** · key `united-states.oregon.southern-oregon.rogue-valley.applegate-valley` · AVA

**Description.** An AVA in the Siskiyou Mountains of Jackson and Josephine counties, entirely within the Rogue Valley AVA, running about 50 miles north from the California border to the Rogue River near Grants Pass. The surrounding ranges shelter it from cool Pacific winds. Most vineyards lie on stream terraces and alluvial fans shaped by the Applegate River.

**Climate.** Hot days and cool nights, with vineyards at up to about 2,000 feet, sheltered from marine air by the mountains around it.

**Soils.** Deep, well-drained stream sediments on terraces and alluvial fans laid down by the Applegate River.

**Grape varieties (text).** Merlot, Cabernet Sauvignon and Syrah lead, with Chardonnay, Tempranillo and Pinot Noir.

**Wine styles (text).** Full-bodied red wines in Bordeaux and Rhône styles from Merlot, Cabernet Sauvignon and Syrah, and white wine from Chardonnay.

- Jackson and Josephine counties

## United States › Oregon › Southern Oregon › Umpqua Valley

Name: **Umpqua Valley** · key `united-states.oregon.southern-oregon.umpqua-valley` · AVA

**Description.** An AVA entirely within Douglas County in southwest Oregon, within the Southern Oregon AVA, known for the 'hundred valleys' formed where three mountain ranges meet. German immigrants grew grapes here from the 1880s, and in 1961 Richard Sommer founded HillCrest Vineyard, where he planted Oregon's first Pinot Noir vines. It contains the Elkton Oregon and Red Hill Douglas County, Oregon AVAs.

**Climate.** Three broad zones: the cool, marine-influenced north around Elkton, with about 50 inches of rain a year; a central zone near Roseburg that suits both cool- and warm-climate grapes; and a warmer, drier south.

**Soils.** Stream sediments, marine sedimentary bedrock and volcanic soils, changing from one small valley to the next.

**Grape varieties (text).** Pinot Noir and Pinot Gris lead, with Syrah, Tempranillo, Merlot and Albariño.

**Wine styles (text).** Red wines from Pinot Noir, Syrah, Tempranillo and Merlot, and white wines from Pinot Gris and Albariño.

**Key facts**

- Established 1984 (27 CFR 9.89)
- Douglas County
- About 3,605 acres planted (Oregon Wine Board)
- HillCrest Vineyard, founded by Richard Sommer in 1961

## United States › Oregon › Southern Oregon › Umpqua Valley › Red Hill Douglas County, Oregon

Name: **Red Hill Douglas County, Oregon** · key `united-states.oregon.southern-oregon.umpqua-valley.red-hill-douglas-county-oregon` · AVA

**Description.** A small AVA of about 5,600 acres around a single hill east of Interstate 5 near Rice Hill, between Yoncalla and Oakland in Douglas County, within the Umpqua Valley AVA. The Applegate and Scott pioneer families settled at its base in the mid-1800s, and Jesse Applegate planted Douglas County's first vineyard nearby, in Yoncalla, in 1876.

**Climate.** Vineyards at 800 to 1,200 feet, without the shelter of the Coast Range, grow cooler than most of Southern Oregon.

**Soils.** Iron-rich, red volcanic Jory soils formed from uplifted ancient basalt.

**Grape varieties (text).** Pinot Noir and Pinot Gris are the predominant grapes.

**Wine styles (text).** Red wine from Pinot Noir, leaner and more structured than elsewhere in Southern Oregon, and white wine from Pinot Gris.

**Key facts**

- Established 2005 (27 CFR 9.190)
- Douglas County
- About 460 acres planted (Oregon Wine Board)
- Its boundary follows the 800-foot contour line (27 CFR 9.190)

## United States › Oregon › The Rocks District of Milton-Freewater

Name: **The Rocks District of Milton-Freewater** · key `united-states.oregon.the-rocks-district-of-milton-freewater` · AVA

**Description.** An AVA of about 3,770 acres near Milton-Freewater in Umatilla County, wholly in Oregon. It lies within the Walla Walla Valley AVA, which in turn lies within the Columbia Valley AVA, on a gently sloping alluvial fan of the Walla Walla River covered in basalt cobbles. Italian immigrants grew grapes here from the 1860s, and modern planting began in the 1990s.

**Climate.** Sunny summers with wide swings between day and night and occasional days over 100 °F, at elevations of about 800 to 1,000 feet.

**Soils.** About 96% Freewater series soils, very cobbly loam and gravelly silt loam full of loose basalt gravel, cobbles and boulders; the stones encourage deep roots and radiate heat to the grapes.

**Grape varieties (text).** Syrah leads, with Cabernet Sauvignon and Grenache.

**Wine styles (text).** Red wines, chiefly Syrah, with Cabernet Sauvignon and Grenache.

## United States › Oregon › Willamette Valley › Chehalem Mountains

Name: **Chehalem Mountains** · key `united-states.oregon.willamette-valley.chehalem-mountains` · AVA

**Description.** An AVA of about 62,500 acres 19 miles southwest of Portland, in Clackamas, Washington and Yamhill counties, within the Willamette Valley AVA. The Chehalem Mountains are a single landmass of hilltops, ridges and spurs rising to 1,633 feet at Bald Peak. Dick Erath planted its first vineyard in 1968; it contains the Ribbon Ridge and Laurelwood District AVAs.

**Climate.** Cool, with the mountains sheltering many vineyards from Columbia Gorge winds; most vineyards lie between 200 and 1,000 feet.

**Soils.** Three main soil types: volcanic basalt, marine sediment, and wind-blown loess of the Laurelwood series.

**Grape varieties (text).** Pinot Noir leads, with Chardonnay, Pinot Gris, Riesling, Pinot Blanc and Gamay.

**Wine styles (text).** Red wines from Pinot Noir and Gamay, and white wines from Chardonnay, Pinot Gris, Riesling and Pinot Blanc.

## United States › Oregon › Willamette Valley › Chehalem Mountains › Laurelwood District

Name: **Laurelwood District** · key `united-states.oregon.willamette-valley.chehalem-mountains.laurelwood-district` · AVA

**Description.** An AVA of about 33,600 acres on the north- and east-facing slopes of the Chehalem Mountains, in Washington and Yamhill counties, entirely within the Chehalem Mountains and Willamette Valley AVAs. It is defined by the near-uniform presence of Laurelwood soil, and it includes the highest point in the Willamette Valley, 1,633 feet.

**Climate.** Much like the rest of the northern Willamette Valley, with vineyards between 200 and 1,000 feet on hilly to mountainous ground.

**Soils.** Laurelwood soil throughout: a base of basalt about 15 million years old topped by wind-blown silt (loess) laid down over the last 200,000 years, up to about 4 feet deep depending on elevation.

**Grape varieties (text).** Pinot Noir leads, with Pinot Gris and Chardonnay.

**Wine styles (text).** Red wine from Pinot Noir and white wines from Pinot Gris and Chardonnay.

## United States › Oregon › Willamette Valley › Chehalem Mountains › Ribbon Ridge

Name: **Ribbon Ridge** · key `united-states.oregon.willamette-valley.chehalem-mountains.ribbon-ridge` · AVA

**Description.** An AVA of about 3,500 acres on a ridge in northern Yamhill County, northwest of Dundee, within the Chehalem Mountains AVA. The ridge stands above the Chehalem Valley floor like an island. Harry Peterson-Nedry planted Ridgecrest Vineyards here in 1980.

**Climate.** The ridge's island-like position shelters it from weather extremes: it warms earlier in the day, cools less at night and escapes the sharpest midsummer heat, giving a long, cool growing season.

**Soils.** Marine sediments of the Willakenzie series, younger, finer and more uniform than the sedimentary and volcanic soils around the ridge; well drained and moderately deep.

**Grape varieties (text).** Pinot Noir leads, with Chardonnay, Pinot Gris, Riesling and Gamay.

## United States › Oregon › Willamette Valley › Dundee Hills

Name: **Dundee Hills** · key `united-states.oregon.willamette-valley.dundee-hills` · AVA

**Description.** An AVA of hills near Newberg in Yamhill County, entirely within the Willamette Valley AVA; its boundary follows the 200-foot contour line where the hills rise from the flat valley floor. Oregon's wine pioneers planted here from 1965, among them David Lett, Dick Erath, the Knudsens and the Sokol Blossers.

## United States › Oregon › Willamette Valley › Mount Pisgah, Polk County, Oregon

Name: **Mount Pisgah, Polk County, Oregon** · key `united-states.oregon.willamette-valley.mount-pisgah-polk-county-oregon` · AVA

**Description.** An AVA of about 5,850 acres around a single mountain in Polk County, within the Willamette Valley AVA, rising from 260 feet at its base to 835 feet at the peak. 'Polk County, Oregon' is part of the name because at least three other places in Oregon are called Mount Pisgah.

**Climate.** Cooler than the land nearby, averaging about 2,543 growing degree days against 2,903 at Salem, and much less windy, at an average of 2.3 mph against 6.1 mph at Salem.

Name: **Tualatin Hills** · key `united-states.oregon.willamette-valley.tualatin-hills` · AVA

**Description.** An AVA of about 144,000 acres in the northwest corner of the Willamette Valley, in Multnomah and Washington counties, entirely within the Willamette Valley AVA, named for and largely defined by the watershed of the Tualatin River. It is sheltered by the Coast Range to the west and the Chehalem Mountains to the south.

**Climate.** A rain shadow gives cooler springs and drier autumns at harvest, with wider day-night swings than the areas around it; vineyards lie between 200 and 1,000 feet.

## United States › Oregon › Willamette Valley › Van Duzer Corridor

Name: **Van Duzer Corridor** · key `united-states.oregon.willamette-valley.van-duzer-corridor` · AVA

**Description.** An AVA of about 59,850 acres in Polk and Yamhill counties, within the Willamette Valley AVA, at the eastern end of a low wind gap in the Coast Range through which Pacific air flows into the valley. Its low, gently rolling hills widen from the narrow western gap toward the east.

**Climate.** Steady, strong winds from the ocean, arriving as early as 2 p.m., cool the vineyards; the wind thickens grape skins and reduces fungal pressure, and heat accumulation stays low.

**Soils.** Shallow, well-drained uplifted marine sedimentary loams and silts with alluvial overlay and some uplifted basalt, high in silt and clay, unlike the volcanic and loess soils around it.

**Grape varieties (text).** Pinot Noir leads, with Chardonnay, Pinot Gris, Riesling, Pinot Blanc and Sauvignon Blanc.

**Wine styles (text).** Red wine from Pinot Noir and white wines from Chardonnay, Pinot Gris, Riesling, Pinot Blanc and Sauvignon Blanc.

**Key facts**

- Established 2018 (27 CFR 9.265)
- Polk and Yamhill counties
- 17 commercial vineyards on about 1,000 acres, and 6 wineries (T.D. TTB-155)
- The Van Duzer Corridor is Oregon's lowest pass through the Coast Range

## United States › Oregon › Willamette Valley › Yamhill-Carlton

Name: **Yamhill-Carlton** · key `united-states.oregon.willamette-valley.yamhill-carlton` · AVA

**Description.** An AVA of about 59,000 acres around the hamlets of Carlton and Yamhill in Yamhill and Washington counties, entirely within the Willamette Valley AVA, limited by law to land between 200 and 1,000 feet. Low ridges ring the two towns in a horseshoe, with the North Yamhill River running through. It was named Yamhill-Carlton District until 2010.

**Climate.** Sheltered on three sides: the Coast Range to the west, nearly 3,500 feet high, casts a rain shadow, and the Chehalem Mountains to the north and the Dundee Hills to the east add further protection.

**Grape varieties (text).** Pinot Noir makes up about 80% of plantings, with Chardonnay and Pinot Gris.

**Wine styles (text).** Mostly red wine from Pinot Noir, with white wines from Chardonnay and Pinot Gris.

**Key facts**

- Established 2004 (27 CFR 9.183)
- Yamhill and Washington counties
- Limited to land between 200 and 1,000 feet (27 CFR 9.183)
- About 2,844 acres planted in 127 vineyards (Willamette Valley Wineries Association)
