## United States › California › Central Coast › Santa Ynez Valley › Happy Canyon of Santa Barbara

Name: **Happy Canyon of Santa Barbara** · key `united-states.california.central-coast.santa-ynez-valley.happy-canyon-of-santa-barbara` · AVA

**Description.** The far eastern end of the Santa Ynez Valley, northeast of Santa Ynez near Lake Cachuma, about 23,900 acres of foothills rising from 500 to about 3,430 feet. It is the warmest part of the valley and grows mainly Bordeaux varieties. "of Santa Barbara" was added to the name because the United States has several other Happy Canyons.

**Climate.** The warmest part of the Santa Ynez Valley, about 3,340 growing degree days: a north–south ridge blocks the Pacific breezes, and rainfall averages about 16 inches.

**Soils.** Alluvial soils below and upland soils above, with green serpentine parent material that gives high magnesium levels, and loams and clay loams with chert and serpentine cobbles.

**Grape varieties (text).** Cabernet Sauvignon, Sauvignon Blanc, Cabernet Franc, Merlot and Petit Verdot, with Syrah and other Rhône varieties.

**Wine styles (text).** Dry Bordeaux-style reds and dry Sauvignon Blanc.

**Key facts**

- Established 2009 (27 CFR 9.217)
- About 23,941 acres, with 492 acres of vineyard in 6 vineyards at establishment (TTB final rule)
- About 3,340 growing degree days (TTB final rule)

Name: **Los Olivos District** · key `united-states.california.central-coast.santa-ynez-valley.los-olivos-district` · AVA

## United States › California › Central Coast › Santa Ynez Valley › Sta. Rita Hills

Name: **Sta. Rita Hills** · key `united-states.california.central-coast.santa-ynez-valley.sta-rita-hills` · AVA

**Description.** The western end of the Santa Ynez Valley between Buellton and Lompoc, a corridor of hills between the Purisima Hills and the Santa Rosa Hills along the Santa Ynez River. Established as Santa Rita Hills, it was renamed Sta. Rita Hills in 2006 after an objection from the Chilean producer Viña Santa Rita. More than 59 vineyards grow about 2,700 acres, mostly Pinot Noir and Chardonnay.

**Climate.** Cool, Region I: fog in the mornings and afternoon ocean breezes; the establishing rule found it markedly cooler during ripening than the Santa Ynez Valley to the east.

**Soils.** A high share of alluvial and marine sands, with sedimentary soils and patches of diatomaceous earth and limestone.

**Grape varieties (text).** Pinot Noir and Chardonnay, with Syrah among some 18 other varieties.

**Wine styles (text).** Dry Pinot Noir and Chardonnay, and cool-climate Syrah.

**Key facts**

- Established 2001 (27 CFR 9.162)
- Renamed from Santa Rita Hills to Sta. Rita Hills, effective 2006 (T.D. TTB-37)
- Over 59 vineyards on about 2,700 acres (Santa Barbara Vintners)
- Elevations from sea level to about 1,800 feet (TTB final rule)

## United States › California › Central Valley › Lodi

Name: **Lodi** · key `united-states.california.central-valley.lodi` · AVA

**Description.** A large AVA at the northern end of the San Joaquin Valley, around the city of Lodi in San Joaquin and Sacramento counties, beside the Sacramento–San Joaquin River Delta. It grows more than 100 varieties, but Zinfandel leads: Lodi crushes about 40% of California's Zinfandel, and it has many old vines. In 2006 seven smaller AVAs were established within it.

**Climate.** Mediterranean: warm days and cool evenings, when the "Delta breeze" draws cool air from San Francisco Bay through the Delta, even in high summer.

**Soils.** Deep, well-drained sandy loams around the city of Lodi in the west, and older clays and cobbly volcanic soils in the eastern foothills.

**Grape varieties (text).** Zinfandel above all, with Cabernet Sauvignon, Chardonnay, Merlot, Petite Sirah and Syrah among more than 100 varieties.

**Wine styles (text).** Dry red wines, above all Zinfandel, dry whites and dry rosé.

**Key facts**

- Established 1986 (27 CFR 9.107)
- Seven AVAs were established within it in 2006 (TTB final rule, 2006)
- Crushes about 40% of California's Zinfandel (Lodi Winegrape Commission)
- Lodi Rules (2005) was California's first third-party-certified sustainable winegrowing program

## United States › California › North Coast › Clear Lake

Name: **Clear Lake** · key `united-states.california.north-coast.clear-lake` · AVA

**Description.** The largest AVA in Lake County, nearly 220,000 acres around Clear Lake, California's largest natural freshwater lake wholly within the state. Vineyards sit high, from about 1,300 feet at the lakeshore to the slopes of Mount Konocti and beyond. It holds Red Hills Lake County, Big Valley District, Kelsey Bench and Upper Lake Valley.

## United States › California › North Coast › Napa Valley

Name: **Napa Valley** · key `united-states.california.north-coast.napa-valley` · AVA

**Description.** California's first AVA, a valley about 30 miles long between the Mayacamas Mountains to the west and the Vaca Range to the east, in Napa County. ## United States › California › North Coast › Napa Valley › Diamond Mountain District

Name: **Diamond Mountain District** · key `united-states.california.north-coast.napa-valley.diamond-mountain-district` · AVA

**Description.** A mountain AVA in the Mayacamas range just south of Calistoga, at the northwest end of Napa Valley. Its name comes from shards of volcanic glass in the soil. Jacob Schram planted vines here in 1862. Wines labelled Diamond Mountain District must also carry the Napa Valley name.

**Climate.** Mostly above the fog line: summer days rarely pass 90 °F and run 10 to 15 °F cooler than on the valley floor, with smaller swings between day and night; Diamond Creek's drainage brings cool air.

**Soils.** Reddish, fine-grained volcanic soils, sometimes gritty, weathered from uplifted volcanic and sedimentary rock; porous and quick-draining.

**Grape varieties (text).** Cabernet Sauvignon dominates, with Cabernet Franc, Merlot and Petit Verdot.

**Wine styles (text).** Dry red wines, chiefly Cabernet Sauvignon and Bordeaux-style blends.

**Key facts**

- Established 2001 (27 CFR 9.166)
- Elevations from about 400 to 2,200 feet (Napa Valley Vintners)
- Named for the reflective volcanic glass in its soils (Napa Valley Vintners)
- Labels must also show "Napa Valley" (Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code §25240)

## United States › California › North Coast › Napa Valley › Howell Mountain

Name: **Howell Mountain** · key `united-states.california.north-coast.napa-valley.howell-mountain` · AVA

**Description.** A plateau in the Vaca Range above St. Helena, around the village of Angwin, and the first AVA established inside Napa Valley. Its southern edge was set at 1,400 feet, where the fog tops out, so its vineyards lie above the valley's fog. Wines labelled Howell Mountain must also carry the Napa Valley name.

**Climate.** Above the fog line on the eastern side of the valley: warmer and drier than much of Napa Valley, with more hours of sun and little marine influence.

**Soils.** Shallow volcanic soils of low fertility that drain freely, from iron-rich red soils to white compressed volcanic ash (tuff).

**Grape varieties (text).** Cabernet Sauvignon above all, with Merlot, Zinfandel and Cabernet Franc; a little Chardonnay and Viognier.

**Wine styles (text).** Dry red wines, mostly Cabernet Sauvignon and Zinfandel, and a few whites.

**Key facts**

- Established 1983 (27 CFR 9.94)
- The first sub-appellation of Napa Valley (Napa Valley Vintners)
- Elevations from about 1,400 to 2,600 feet (Napa Valley Vintners)
- Labels must also show "Napa Valley" (Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code §25240)

## United States › California › North Coast › Napa Valley › Mt. Veeder

Name: **Mt. Veeder** · key `united-states.california.north-coast.napa-valley.mt-veeder` · AVA

**Description.** A mountain AVA on the eastern slopes of the Mayacamas range, northwest of the city of Napa, along the line between Napa and Sonoma. It is one of the larger Napa sub-AVAs but among the least planted, because the ground is steep and wooded. It is named after the 1850s pastor Peter Veeder. Wines labelled Mt. Veeder must also carry the Napa Valley name.

**Climate.** Cool for Napa: summer days run 10 to 15 °F below the valley floor, with mid-summer highs around 85 °F, and most vineyards sit above the fog line.

**Soils.** Shallow, acidic, sandy and sandy-loam soils of marine sedimentary origin, an old seabed with Franciscan mélange beneath and rare volcanic pockets; well drained and low in fertility.

**Grape varieties (text).** Cabernet Sauvignon above all, with Zinfandel, Merlot, Chardonnay and Syrah.

**Wine styles (text).** Dry red wines, mainly Cabernet Sauvignon and Zinfandel, and dry Chardonnay.

**Key facts**

- Established 1990 (27 CFR 9.123)
- Elevations from about 500 to 2,600 feet; most above 1,400 feet (Napa Valley Vintners)
- Over 1,000 acres planted (Napa Valley Vintners)
- Labels must also show "Napa Valley" (Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code §25240)
